The guide

Knee rules

Log your knee score the morning after each run (0 = nothing, 10 = worst). The score decides the next step, not the plan.

0–2Carry on as planned. 3–4Repeat this week's volume next week instead of progressing. Swap the next run for the bike if it is still there. 5+Stop running for 3–5 days, keep the strength work pain-free, and book a physio if it doesn't settle.

Stop and get it checked if you get locking, catching, the knee giving way, or swelling that is there the next morning. Those are meniscus signs, not training soreness.

  • Before week 1, a single physio or sports-medicine visit is the best money you will spend on this.
  • Soft, flat surfaces first: grass pitches, park paths, a lit track. Avoid steep downhills until the base phase is done.
  • Short, quick steps (around 170–180 per minute) load the knee less than long strides.
  • Rotate two pairs of shoes from spring and retire them around 600–800 km.

Isometric holds settle sore knees; do them first. Bodyweight in phase 1, add a loaded backpack or dumbbells from phase 2, then go heavier for 4 × 6 in phases 3–4.

Effort guide

Paces come from effort (RPE, 1–10) rather than a watch until your half marathon in week 43 sets a goal pace.

Easy: full sentences, nose breathing possibleRPE 3–4
Marathon effort: steady, a few words at a timeRPE 5–6
Tempo: comfortably hard, short phrasesRPE 6–7
Intervals: hard but controlled, last rep as good as the firstRPE 7–8
Strides: 20 s quick and relaxed, not a sprintRPE 8

Football fitness makes hard efforts feel natural and easy running feel pointless. Around 80% of the minutes should be easy; that is what builds the engine and spares the knees.

Running in the dark, around the kids
  • Lay kit out the night before, shoes by the door, head torch charged.
  • Head torch plus a red rear light and something reflective on the moving parts (ankles, wrists).
  • Pick one lit, flat loop near home for weekday runs. A treadmill run counts the same.
  • Early mornings: 5 minutes of leg swings, bridges and calf raises indoors first. Joints are stiffest straight out of bed; never start a quality session cold.
  • Late evenings: keep tempo and interval sessions at least 2 hours before bed or sleep suffers; move them to mornings if so.
  • Long runs go out at first light on Saturday and are back before breakfast. Swap Saturday and Sunday freely to suit the family.
  • Miss a session? Skip it, don't stack it. Miss a week? Repeat the previous week.
